Versus the PLSA Retirement Living Standards

Pensions UK (formerly the Pension and Lifetime Savings Association, PLSA) publishes three annual spending levels people say they need for a Minimum, Moderate or Comfortable retirement. These are spending targets, not gross income, so your projected net income at your current age is compared against them directly, in today's money, 2025 figures.

This is an illustration, not financial or tax advice. It is meant to help you explore the trade-offs between withdrawal rate, longevity and tax, not to replace guidance from a regulated financial adviser, MoneyHelper's free Pension Wise service, or HMRC.

Every year's withdrawal is a fixed real amount, set once at the outset as the chosen withdrawal rate multiplied by today's combined pot, and held flat in today's money for as long as the pot lasts (the classic "safe withdrawal rate" approach). It does not adjust for how investments actually perform year to year, and takes no account of sequence-of-returns risk — a run of poor early returns could exhaust the pot sooner than shown here, even at the same average growth rate.

25% of the pension pot is assumed to be tax-free cash; the remaining 75%, plus any State Pension once it starts, is taxed as income using 2026/27 Personal Allowance and Income Tax bands (or the Scottish bands, if selected), with the standard high-income taper above £100,000. National Insurance is not charged on pension or ISA income. The Personal Allowance and tax bands are held flat in real terms for this projection; in practice they have been frozen in cash terms in recent years, so in a genuinely static-policy future they would be worth progressively less, and real-world tax could be higher than shown here the further out the projection runs.

The State Pension is assumed to increase in line with inflation (a simplification of the triple lock) and so is also held flat in today's money once it starts. The full new State Pension is currently £241.30 a week (about £12,548 a year); your own entitlement depends on your National Insurance record and may differ — check your forecast at gov.uk/check-state-pension.

The Retirement Living Standards figures used are Pensions UK's 2025 update, and are national (non-London) figures. They represent annual household spending, not gross income, and don't assume any particular tax treatment.
