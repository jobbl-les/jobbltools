Gilt Ladder Calculator

Build an illustrative UK gilt ladder — staggered maturities and coupon dates — from a lump sum, then see the full buy instructions and cash-flow schedule.

Illustrative planning tool — not investment or tax advice, not a dealing ticket. The "buy instructions" below are a proposed allocation against a price snapshot, not a live order. Prices come from the same daily London Stock Exchange snapshot used by the Gilt Cashflow & Tax Calculator — see the data timestamp at the bottom of the page. Dealing/platform charges are excluded entirely from every figure here; at smaller ladder sizes with many rungs, per-line dealing costs are exactly where a real ladder becomes less attractive than this projection suggests. Only conventional (fixed-coupon) gilts are considered — index-linked gilts are excluded because their future coupons depend on RPI inflation between now and each payment date, which can't be projected deterministically. Selection uses a greedy heuristic, not a formal optimiser — see "How this works" at the bottom of the page.

How this works

  • Selection is a greedy heuristic, not a formal optimiser. A proper constrained optimisation (LP/MILP) over gilt selection + weighting would be strictly more optimal, especially once frequency and yield are blended, but needs a solver this static, no-build, no-dependency site doesn't have. Instead, each rung is filled slot-by-slot (evenly spaced target maturities across the horizon), scoring candidates on maturity fit, return, and whether they add a new coupon-month to the ladder — see ladder-calc.js for the exact scoring.
  • Tax treatment is a heuristic tilt, not a real after-tax calculation. Gilts are exempt from Capital Gains Tax on redemption (TCGA 1992 s.115); coupon income is taxable as savings income outside a wrapper. For a taxable account, selection favours annualised capital-gain-to-maturity over yield-to-maturity (a fixed 70/30 tilt); ISA/SIPP accounts see no tilt since neither component is taxed there. This doesn't know your marginal rate or use any actual allowance figures.
  • Rolling ladders reinvest at today's snapshot price. When a rung matures before the horizon end, its proceeds are modelled as immediately redeployed into a new gilt targeting a similarly-spaced future maturity — using that gilt's current price, since no future price is knowable. The still-open position at the horizon end is reported separately, not forced to a fictitious final redemption.
  • HMRC's Accrued Income Scheme isn't modelled — every coupon shown as receivable is shown in full, though in reality the pre-purchase slice of a mid-period buyer's first coupon is taxed as the seller's income, not the buyer's (same simplification as the Gilt Cashflow & Tax Calculator).
  • Blended yield-to-maturity/IRR is solved across every buy cost and every projected cashflow at once (semi-annual compounding), not just an average of each gilt's own yield. For a rolling ladder, any position still open at the horizon end is marked to market at today's snapshot price on that date (not assumed to be worth its full face value, since it hasn't matured yet) purely so a single comparable figure exists — it isn't a realised cashflow.
